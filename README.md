Overview

This project investigates the core drivers of bank customer churn by testing three competing hypotheses:

Low customer engagement
Poor service experience
High or unexpected fees

The goal is to determine which factor has the greatest relative impact on churn, giving banking institutions clearer guidance on where to focus retention efforts.

Approach

Four independently sourced datasets were profiled, cleaned, and integrated through entity resolution to build a unified view of customer behavior across activity, satisfaction, and financial dimensions.

The analysis combines:

Descriptive EDA to explore each dimension individually
Diagnostic statistical testing (Pearson correlation, t-tests) and logistic regression to rank individual churn drivers
ROC-AUC evaluation to compare the overall predictive power of each dimension

The end goal is to translate these findings into data-driven, targeted retention strategies for banking institutions.
